## 1. THE BIGGEST DESIGN FACT IN THE WHOLE SYSTEM

**IBJJF no-gi competition rash guards must have a black, white, or black-and-white base, and
must show at least 10% of the athlete's belt-rank colour.** Rank colours are white, blue,
purple, brown, black. Non-compliance is a disqualification, not a warning.

Sponsor and logo artwork may not exceed 50% of the surface. Short and long sleeve both legal.

**Why this reframes everything:**

- The board has been treating base colour as a free creative choice. **For the competition
  segment it is not a choice at all.** Khaki, burgundy, olive, jade — all illegal at IBJJF.
- It explains why every competitor's best-seller is black, and why "Ranked" is a whole product
  category across Hayabusa, Sanabul, Scramble and others.
- **It relocates where design freedom actually lives:** not in the base colour, but in
  *texture, graphic, tonal work and where the 10% rank colour is placed.* Black-on-black
  texture is not a stylistic preference in this market — it is the main available move.
- ADCC and most submission-only events are **permissive** — patterned and bright kit is fine.
  So the ruleset splits the range in two, and that split should be a deliberate product
  decision rather than an accident.

> **⚠️ Live gap:** Future Kimonos has **one** ranked series (Cosmic Ranked) and it ships in
> White and **Red** — red is not an IBJJF rank colour. Only 3 of 250 products mention a belt
> or IBJJF anywhere. For a brand whose own site trades on *"competition tested"* and
> *"Earn the Glory"*, that is a commercial gap, not just a design one.

## 2. Who the customer actually is

| | |
|---|---|
| **Starts training at** | ~29 |
| **Blue belt** | ~32 · **Purple** ~35 · **Brown** ~37 · **Black** ~39 |
| **Gender split** | ~90% men / ~10% women |
| **Scale** | ~18m martial arts participants in the US; 450m+ combat sports practitioners globally |
| **Market** | Martial arts wear ~$5.5bn (2024) → ~$8.7bn (2033), ~5.2% CAGR. MMA equipment/merch ~$4.8bn (2025) → ~$7.3bn (2033), ~7.3% CAGR |

**The single most useful number here is the age.** This is a **thirty-something adult market
with disposable income**, not a teen market. The average buyer of a $79 rash guard has been
training for years, has a job, and is buying their fifth one. That argues against loud
juvenile graphics and toward the restraint Ladana's own benchmark already describes.

### The four segments, and they want different things

1. **The competitor.** Trains 4–6×/week, enters IBJJF and ADCC events. **Constrained by ruleset.**
   Buys for legality, fit and looking sharp on the podium. This is FK's stated core.
2. **The serious hobbyist — the volume.** 2–4×/week, may never compete. **Unconstrained.**
   Buys on aesthetics and identity. Wears the brand outside the gym. Shoyoroll's entire market.
3. **The beginner.** Buying their first kit. Buys on price, brand recognition and rank compliance.
   Low margin, high volume, and the least design-led.
4. **The fan.** Doesn't necessarily train. Buys because Gordon Ryan wears it. **This is the
   athlete-collection mechanic FK already runs**, and it is the one segment where the design
   matters *less* than whose name is on it.

**Inference, flagged as such:** FK's $79 price and made-to-order model points at segments 1, 2
and 4, not 3.

## 3. What actually sells — direct evidence, not opinion

**Read off Venum's own popularity sort, 7 Aug 2026** (compression shirts / rash guards).
Venum is UFC's exclusive outfitter with 1.7m Instagram followers, so this is the closest thing
to a market-wide read available.

The most popular items are, almost without exception:

- **Black base.** Overwhelmingly. Colourway names are *Stealth*, *Spectre* — black/white,
  black/khaki, black/red.
- **Tonal black-on-black** panelling and texture, with thin grey or gold seam lines.
- **When there is a graphic, it is on the BACK ONLY** and in a single muted colour — one dragon
  in dusty blue, front left plain.
- **One near-plain long-sleeve** with nothing but a small chest logo sits in the top handful.
- Price NZ$87–96 (≈ FK's $79 tier).

**This is Ladana's observation confirmed: the popular designs are simple.**

**And it independently validates FK's own best instinct.** Serpent Core is described in FK's
own catalogue as *"a clean aesthetic on the front and a glitched snake image on the back"* —
which is precisely the structure of Venum's top sellers, and precisely the *front quiet, back
loud* mechanic in `BENCHMARK.md`. Three independent sources now agree.

## 4. What the market says it wants

Ranked by how much evidence sits behind each, so weak claims are visibly weak.

| Claim | Confidence | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Black base dominates | **High** | IBJJF rule + Venum popularity sort + FK's own catalogue |
| Front quiet / back loud | **High** | Venum top sellers + FK Serpent Core + benchmark |
| Restraint reads as premium | **High** | Shoyoroll's entire positioning; minimal branding, limited drops |
| Fit, durability, flatlock stitching and sweat-wicking are table stakes | **High** | Every competitor's own product copy leads on these before design |
| Tonal / monotone over multi-colour | **Medium-high** | Venum sort + Ladana's own keeps |
| Animal hide texture is live | **Medium** | Ladana kept croc twice and snake once; Venum ships leopard and snake |
| Buyers want it wearable outside the gym | **Medium** | Shoyoroll's "mat to café" positioning |
| Bright / multi-colour has a market | **Low–medium** | Exists at ADCC and in athlete kit, but not in the popularity data |

### What is table stakes, not differentiation

Every competitor leads their copy on **4-way stretch, flatlock seams, sweat-wicking, mesh
underarm panels, IBJJF legality**. Nobody wins on these — but a premium brand that omits them
from its product copy loses. Worth checking FK's product pages say all five.

## 6. What this means for the board — apply these when scoring

1. **Stop treating base colour as free.** For anything competition-facing, black/white + 10%
   rank colour. A find whose whole idea is an unusual base colour is a *training/ADCC* idea —
   file it, but say which.
2. **Weight black-on-black texture up.** It is the main legal move in the biggest segment, and
   the popularity data says the market rewards it. Ladana kept the all-texture Venum Rage.
3. **Front quiet, back loud is now evidence-backed**, not taste. Treat a find that demonstrates
   it as more valuable than a find that is merely pretty.
4. **The 10% rank colour is a design opportunity nobody is using well.** Every competitor
   treats it as a compliance panel. It could be the signature element — a rank-coloured line
   tracing the seams, in the gold-as-line mechanic Ladana has now kept three times.
5. **Judge against a 35-year-old with money**, not a teenager. That is the actual buyer.

## 7. Open questions worth answering next

1. **Does FK want the IBJJF competition segment properly?** One non-compliant ranked series
   suggests not, but the brand messaging says yes. This is a strategy question, not a design one.
2. **What do practitioners actually call "cheap"?** The board has Ladana's answer. It does not
   have the market's.
3. **How much of the customer base competes at all?** Changes the legal/illegal range split.
4. **Does the fan segment (Gordon Ryan buyers) care about design at all**, or only the name?
   If only the name, athlete collections should be judged on a different bar entirely.
