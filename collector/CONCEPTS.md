# Five collection concepts — 7 August 2026

Built from what Ladana actually kept and rejected across 44 finds, not invented from scratch.
Every one traces to specific board ids.

**None of these is a design.** Each is a *spine* — a name, a palette, a motif system and a
layout rule — that a designer or an image tool can work from. The board is the parts bin;
this is the first assembly.

**Why five different ones rather than five variations:** they deliberately sit at different
points on two axes — loud vs quiet, and illustrated vs textural. If one gets built, the others
should still be worth building.

# 2 · HIDE

**The one your review most clearly asked for, and the least like anything in the catalogue.**

**Spine.** Animal skin as terrain, not as a creature. No snake, no crocodile, no animal
depicted anywhere — only the *surface*. Scale, hide, shed skin, armour lamellae. The garment
becomes the animal rather than carrying a picture of one.

**Why this one.** You kept croc and snake and wrote *"like the idea of using animal print"*.
Both were **black on black, tonal** — reading as texture at distance and only resolving up
close. That is the mechanic, and it is the opposite of how Future Kimonos has used the serpent
so far: Serpent Core draws a snake, this becomes one.

**Palette.** Black on black, three values of near-black separated by sheen alone. One
colourway only at launch. If it needs an accent, a single cold metallic — never red.

**Motif system.** One scale geometry, scaled at three sizes across the body: fine on the
sleeves, medium on the torso, coarse across the shoulders. Same shape, three densities — the
mechanic from the Venum desert camo (`F0807-01`), applied to hide instead of camo.

**Layout.** No hero graphic at all. This is the one piece in the range with nothing on the
back. The whole design is surface.

**Board:** `F0806-29` croc · `F0806-28` snake · `F0807-08` Matupa · `F0807-01` three-density camo
**Position:** quiet, textural. **The direct counterweight to Horse.**
**Risk:** with no hero, this lives or dies on print quality. Sample before committing.

# 3 · ONE STROKE

**Spine.** A single brush gesture as the entire back panel. Calligraphy where the mark has
stopped being language and become pure form — the letter abandoned, the movement kept.

**Why this one.** Your calligraphy references (`F0807-11/12/13`) were the fastest, clearest
reaction you have had to anything: *"I love the gradient, patterns and prints."* And your
benchmark already says **kanji as a graphic element, not as text** — this is that principle
taken to its end.

**The culture fix.** Your three references are almost certainly Islamic devotional works.
Sacred script on a fight garment would cause real offence. `F0807-20` — a 14th-century Japanese
Zen scroll from the Met — carries the identical mechanics in a script the brand can legitimately
claim. **Build from that one.**

**Palette.** Black and red on cream. Your own brand colours, arrived at from a completely
different direction.

**Motif system.** Two densities of one language, straight from your red/black reference: a
dense, low-contrast field of small marks across the torso, and **one enormous stroke** over
the top. Dry-brush breakup where the ink runs out — a texture no vector tool makes and one
that survives sublimation.

**Layout.** Back: one stroke, floor to shoulder, cropped by the seams on both sides. Front:
the dense field only, no hero. Sleeves: empty.

**Board:** `F0807-20` Zen scroll · `F0807-11/12/13` calligraphy · `F0807-22` ink-density landscape
**Position:** loud, gestural — but *one* loud thing.

# 4 · BOKASHI

**Spine.** The whole design is a vertical fade with exactly one hot accent in it. Named for the
hand-wiped ink gradient Japanese printers used, which is what it is.

**Why this one.** Two pieces of evidence point the same way. You wrote *"like the flame
gradients for a background print"* on `F0806-03`. And the trade finding on record says
**dark-to-light vertical torso fades are the most-requested sublimation effect of the year** —
so this is commercially current, not just pretty.

**The crucial detail.** `F0806-05` proves a gradient alone is wallpaper. That print works
because one small red-robed figure sits in an otherwise empty field and gives the fade
something to be measured against. **The accent is the design; the gradient is the stage.**

**Palette.** Sampled, not invented — `F0806-06`, read off the print pixel by pixel: indigo-black
`#303040` at the hem rising to paper `#F0F0E0` at the shoulder, with flame `#F0B070` as the
single accent. No pure black, no pure white, no saturated red.

**Motif system.** Almost nothing. Speckle texture over the fade. One small saturated element,
placed off-centre and high.

**Layout.** Fade runs hem-to-shoulder, dark at the bottom. One accent at roughly the shoulder
blade. Everything else empty.

**Board:** `F0806-05` bokashi · `F0806-06` sampled palette · `F0806-03` flame · `F0807-13` gold sweep
**Position:** quiet, atmospheric.
**Note:** this is the closest thing to James's stated preference for restraint that has ever
been on this board — and it arrives as *premium* rather than as *plain*.

# 5 · DESERT PROTOCOL

**Spine.** A full collection with **no black in it anywhere.** Military neutrals — khaki,
forest, sand, bone — treated with technical micro-typography instead of graphics.

**Why this one.** You kept two warm-neutral pieces (`F0807-01`, `F0807-09`) and wrote *"like
the colourway"* and *"really like this colourway"*. Both have **zero black**. In a category
that defaults to black on every rack, this is the cheapest possible way to look different, and
you found it twice without prompting.

**Motif system.** Barely any illustration. Instead: the technical label vocabulary from the
Venum desert piece — small boxed codes, batch numbers, `FW3`-style tags — used as **texture at
6pt**, exactly as Serpent Core already uses its footer. Plus the three-density shard camo,
solid and hatched and speckled.

**Layout.** Panels defined by tonal shifts rather than by graphics. The customisation name runs
small and technical rather than as a hero — the inverse of concept 6.

**Board:** `F0807-01` desert camo · `F0807-09` khaki/forest · `F0807-10` matched kit · `F0806-20` olive/pink
**Position:** quiet, technical.
**Commercial note:** the Venum khaki set runs as rash guard *and* shorts *and* tights. This is
the concept most obviously built as a **kit** rather than a single piece.

# 6 · THE NAME

**The one that is a business idea as much as a design idea.**

**Spine.** Personalisation stops being a $5 add-on and becomes the entire design. The buyer's
name runs the full length of the spine in gold. Every garment is one-off by definition.

**Why this one.** `F0807-03` — UFC 288 Champ — does exactly this, and **Future Kimonos already
sells name, handle and country-flag customisation.** The mechanic is already built, already
paid for, and currently used as a small extra rather than as the product.

**The design mechanic you kept twice.** Gold as a **line, not a fill** — tracing panel seams
rather than filling shapes. It appears in both `F0807-03` and `F0807-08`, the only mechanic
present in two separate picks.

**Palette.** Near-black base, gold line, nothing else. Two colours.

**Motif system.** No illustration at all. Type is the graphic: the name vertically down the
spine, a small technical block beneath it, thin gold tracing the seams.

**Layout.** Back is the name, full height. Front is quiet with a small chest lockup — the
Serpent Core "front quiet, back loud" principle already proven in the catalogue.

**Board:** `F0807-03` UFC 288 · `F0807-08` Matupa gold line · `F0806-01` four type registers
**Position:** loud, typographic — no illustration anywhere.
**Why it may matter most:** every other concept needs new artwork for every colourway. This one
generates infinite unique product from a single template, which is a direct answer to eighteen
months without a new series.

## What is deliberately not here

**Country and heritage editions.** The strongest *commercial* idea in `SEEDS.md`, but it is a
data question, not a design one — the answer is in your Shopify orders by country. Guessing
which nations would waste the idea. Pull the numbers and it becomes concept 7.

**Anything Americana or national-colourway.** You cut three of those as off-brand. Deliberately
excluded rather than forgotten.
