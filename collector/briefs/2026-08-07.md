### 2. Synthwave and album art as graphic systems

**Evidence:** Synthwave album covers (electronic/industrial music) solve composition at vertical rectangle scale:
- Neon grids, sunset gradients, chrome lettering, one powerful graphic carrying entire aesthetic
- Designers working in 600×600 and vertical album-cover formats produce the exact type hierarchy problem a rash guard solves
- Cover design teaches: single focal colour event (neon accent), restrained palette (usually 2–3 colours), type as graphic, negative space as compositional tool

**Sources:** ReleasKit guide, Newretro.net, Premade Pixels galleries all demonstrate working examples.

**Direction:** `growing` — This is a reference-class lane that isn't being hunted. Album covers carry the "one powerful graphic" principle and type hierarchy work that benchmark designs hold, without the requirement to find physical garments.

**Relevance:** If synthwave covers are filed as `reference` finds (not products), they contribute composition and colour systems without the construction constraint. Expands what counts as premium reference.

### 3. Showa era + Japanese movie posters as parallel solutions

**Evidence:**
- Showa advertising (1960s–1970s) during Japan's rapid modernization: vibrant, culturally expressive, family-happiness narrative
- Japanese movie posters contemporaneous with ukiyo-e archive finds already on board: master calligraphers created typography, Yokoo used layered imagery and political symbolism, science-fiction posters developed futuristic letterforms
- Both solve the identical problem: hero composition on vertical format, narrative text as texture, calligraphic type as graphic element

**Direction:** `stable` — This is historical confirmation that the editorial-poster structure (title, subtitle, body copy, signature, illustration, logos) is not a modern invention. It's the native tradition for this subject.

**Relevance:** Museums and archives hold thousands of examples. This is high-yield reference territory that product feeds cannot surface. The Takeshi rash guard's structure has 150+ years of precedent in this exact medium.
