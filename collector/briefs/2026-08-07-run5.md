## What the review data said, and what I did about it

**15 kept, 21 cut.** Two things in it were decisive.

**1. Colour is what you keep for.** Nine of fifteen notes name a colour or gradient
relationship — *"like the gradient and colour combination"*, *"Like the colour combination"*
(twice), *"Like the first 2 shades"*, *"Like the way colour is used here"*. Nothing else comes
close. So every find this run has its palette read off the image and stated.

**2. The product half is where the failure is.** Eighteen of twenty-one cuts were product
listings. Reference artwork was cut twice, both crowded multi-figure ukiyo-e. **So this run is
deliberately 70% reference artwork and 30% product** — museum 35%, Wikimedia 35%, product 30%.

> ⚠️ **That breaches the "no source method over about a third" rule, slightly and on purpose.**
> Declaring it rather than hiding it. The justification is your own reject data, not my
> preference. If you would rather see more product next run even at a lower hit rate, say so
> and I will rebalance.

## The three findings worth your attention

**1. The Tatami Contrast reject has an answer.** You cut two-colour blocking as `plain`. The
Peruvian feathered panel (`F0807-32`) is the *same structure* — hard-edged blue and yellow
blocks — and it is magnificent, because the surface is thousands of feathers, so each block
has directional pile and tonal drift. **Colour blocking fails when the colour is flat, not
because it is blocking.** That reframes a standing filter into a solvable problem.

**2. Two independent Javanese sarongs split the garment vertically** (`F0807-33`, `F0807-36`) —
one changes colour *and* pattern across hard seams, the other runs the same motif light-on-dark
then dark-on-light. Two examples from different decades means it is a tradition, not a one-off.
This is the cheapest route to front-quiet/back-loud.

**3. Gold-as-line is not about gold.** You kept it twice. The McLaren Stealth livery
(`F0807-45`) does the identical thing in fluorescent orange, confined to leading edges — nose
tip, wing endplate, wheel rim — so the highlights describe the form and the body stays black.
**The mechanic is placement, not the metal.** On a torso: shoulder seam, collar, cuff.

## Deliberately not filed

Two things were found, looked at, and **left off the board on purpose**:

- **Māori kowhaiwhai** rafter painting — red/black/white koru scrolls, visually superb.
- **Aboriginal Australian dot painting**, and a Kakadu image that turned out to be **Ubirr rock art**.

Both are living cultural property with active protocols against outside commercial use. This is
the S24 caution doing its job. Recording them here so the decision is visible and so nobody
re-finds them in three months and files them by accident.

**Also dropped:** the Art Institute of Chicago. Its API is excellent but it **blocks image
hotlinking entirely**, so nothing from it can appear on the board. Recorded in `SOURCES.md`.
