## What went on the board

Six finds, all `reference` class, all in `printmaking-remix` and `neo-japanese`. Three scored 6/6, three scored 5/6.

They are not a random six. Looked at together they describe **one coherent compositional system**, and it is the system your own Takeshi piece already uses:

1. **A single hero on a mostly empty field.** Kuniyoshi's Akashi Ridayu puts one warrior on cream void. Yoshitoshi's Horse and Rider gives the hero the middle third of a wide sheet and leaves the top and bottom thirds genuinely empty.
2. **Type in four registers at four scales.** Vertical title cartouche, artist signature, red seals, and a dense text block used as *grey texture* rather than as reading matter. This is exactly what Takeshi does with the vertical name, the script signature, the body paragraph and the 6pt caption.
3. **Two colour events maximum on a dark ground.** Yoshitoshi's Kurahashi Densuke is near-black across the whole sheet with exactly two things carrying colour.
4. **Gradient needs one hot accent.** Kuniyoshi's snow print runs a hand-wiped indigo-to-white fade and puts one small red figure in it. Without the red the fade is wallpaper.

**The strongest single observation this run:** the editorial-poster layout Ladana wants is not a modern streetwear import onto Japanese subject matter. Ukiyo-e was already doing it in 1848 — hero, cartouche, body text as texture, seal system. That is a much stronger provenance story for a collection than "we made it look like a magazine cover", and it is defensible in copy.

## Palette, sampled not guessed

`F0806-06` was read off the digitised Yoshitoshi print pixel by pixel. What it *isn't* is the finding: **no pure black, no pure white, no saturated red.** Darkest value is a blue-black `#303040`; every light is a warm paper tone; the only heat is a soft orange `#F0B070`.

Against the house palette — black plus `#ED1F24` — this is a different register: warmer, older, less shouty, still unmistakably Japanese. Worth testing as a limited-edition alternative rather than a replacement.

*Caveat:* sampled from a 140-year-old sheet, so the creams carry paper ageing as well as ink. Treat as direction, not spec.

## The product sweep — what 21 garments say

Four things worth acting on, drawn from the sweep rather than from any single item.

**1. Troy Lee is running the best sublimation in the sweep, by a distance.** The Proton jersey pushes a magenta-to-teal gradient through the torso that *dissolves* before the hem — the gradient has an edge and a direction, so it reads as a shape rather than a background. The Sever jersey does the restrained version: black to grey, tonal, no hue shift. Between them they cover both the loud brand language and the internal preference for deep greys, using one engine.

**2. One artwork, three colourways, three completely different moods.** Sever ships black/grey, white/teal and dark olive/pumice. This is the structural answer to eighteen months without a new series — a single panel geometry can carry a season of drops. Tatami were doing the same thing with Contrast, but with a colour block instead of an artwork, which is why it read as cheap. The mechanic isn't the problem; what you hang on it is.

**3. The kit is the product, not the piece.** Gold ships Aurelius as a matched rash guard and short, and Canarinho as a deliberately *un*-matched pair — yellow top, blue shorts, matched by national reference rather than tone. Both are filed side by side so you can compare the two ways of building a set.

**4. Gold is running place and mythology as repeatable series.** Finest City follows La Ciudad in a San Diego programme; Aurelius runs Roman emperor. Both are stories a customer can explain to someone else, which is exactly what Takeshi does. Note how little illustration is involved — Aurelius is two colours and a script.

**One deliberate floor marker:** `F0806-30`, the plain red Shoyoroll rash guard, has no artwork at all. It is on the board so the gap between it and the Kuniyoshi prints is visible in one scroll. Shoyoroll can sell that on brand equity. Future Kimonos cannot. Reject it and the filter learns where the floor is.

**One thing to decide, not just look at:** Tatami's Snake shorts put snakeskin all over a garment as *texture*. Serpent Core and Cobra 2099 already own the serpent as *illustration*. Whether that overlap is a threat or a confirmation is a call for you and James, not for me.

## What did not go on the board, and why

This is the honest half.

### The curated tier stayed small on purpose

Nothing from the buildable lanes cleared 4/6 as *curated* work — the 21 garments are on the board as sweep, ungated, which is a different claim. If the premium bar had been applied to them, roughly three would have survived.

### Behance apparel search is a dead well — register updated

Searches for `esports jersey design`, `muay thai shorts design` and `cyber samurai apparel graphic` returned, in order: free mockup template downloads, tech packs and flat sketches, and Adobe Stock clipart samurai on white tees. Almost nothing was original design work.

**Why:** Behance's apparel category is dominated by designers advertising production services, not showing art direction. The signal-bearing Behance terms are the *graphic* ones — `print placement`, `techwear branding`, `fashion graphics` — not the *garment* ones. `SOURCES.md` should be amended on the next pass; I have not silently changed it.

### MotoGP livery coverage is video, not stills

The Ducati centenary reveal on motogp.com is a video embed and rider portraits. The Race is behind a pay-or-consent wall — I did not accept cookies to get through it. Motorsport liveries remain a good territory; the route to them needs to be image galleries, not launch articles.

### Cav Empt — excellent, but it stays as prose

C.E.'s lookbook is the closest thing on the open web to the brief, and three mechanics are worth taking:

- **1-bit dither / bitmap halftone as the hero treatment.** Portraits reduced to pure black-and-white dot patterns. Reads as image *and* texture at once, survives any print process, and looks technical rather than decorative.
- **The framed graphic panel.** The artwork sits inside a drawn rectangle with a caption below it, on an otherwise blank body — the opposite of full-bleed. Directly transferable to a back panel.
- **Matched sets sharing a vocabulary.** A blue dither tee shown with blue-striped shorts; a camo fleece with camo trousers. The set is the product, not the piece.

It is prose and not a board image for two reasons: the lookbook is model photography on a shop site rather than artwork, and the page blocks image extraction, so I could not obtain a citable image URL. Filing it with a broken or uncredited image would breach the honesty rules. Source: [cavempt.com/looks](https://www.cavempt.com/looks)

### The Met's open-access Kuniyoshi set is mostly landscape, not warriors

Worth knowing before someone else spends a run on it. His famous tattooed-Suikoden warriors are largely *not* in the Met's public-domain set; what is there is landscape and genre work. **Yoshitoshi is the better-stocked warrior source at the Met** — that is where three of the six finds came from.

1. **Re-point the Behance and motorsport routes** in `SOURCES.md` per the two dead ends above.
2. **Run the buildable lanes properly** — wrestling singlet galleries and UFC/Venum fight-kit programmes were not reached this run, and they are where `buildable` finds should come from.

The question worth answering on this board: does the compositional system hold — hero, cartouche, text-as-texture, two colour events, gradient plus one hot accent — or is it just ukiyo-e being pretty? Star what holds. Reject what doesn't, with a reason, and the reasons become filters.
