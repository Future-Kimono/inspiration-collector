## The one-line brief

> Premium limited-edition technical apparel at the intersection of **Japanese culture, futuristic design, and luxury streetwear** — communicating discipline, mythology, technology and movement through **restrained colour, bold hero artwork, layered typography and thoughtful composition** rather than excessive visual complexity.

Every word of that is load-bearing. *Restrained* colour and *bold* hero artwork are not in tension — the restraint is what makes the hero land.

## The three benchmark files

Stored in `benchmark/`. Colour values are read off the artwork and are close approximations, not spec.

### 1. `02-takeshi-yamaguchi-rashguard.png` — the editorial poster

The clearest statement of the aesthetic in the whole set.

| Element | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Base** | Black across every panel. No exception |
| **Front** | The *same* ukiyo-e scene ghosted back to near-black — texture, not statement. A small centred lockup at chest: red samurai-helmet icon, "100 DOGS OF WAR", "FUTURE KIMONOS" |
| **Back** | Full-colour ukiyo-e warrior and tiger over a torn cream painterly wash. One hero image, occupying roughly the upper two-thirds, resolving into black |
| **Type** | Four registers at once — "TAKESHI" vertical in condensed bold sans; "Yamaguchi" in red brush script overlapping it; a paragraph of serif body copy telling the character's story; "Future Kimonos" as a red script signature |
| **Sleeves** | Almost empty. Fine vertical stripes only |
| **Collar** | Small red-and-white tomoe roundel |

**Palette:** black `#0A0A0A` · red `#ED1F24` · cream `#F0E6D2` · ukiyo-e green `#4A5D4E` · ochre `#C9974B` · white

**The mechanic to steal:** *a poster wrapped around a body.* Title, subtitle, body copy, signature, illustration, logos — magazine cover logic, not jersey logic. And **front quiet, back loud**, which is the Serpent Core principle already in the catalogue.

### 2. `03-serpent-cyan-red-rashguard.png` — glitch and restraint

| Element | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Base** | Black with heavy black-on-black distressed texture — the surface has material quality even where nothing is happening |
| **Palette** | Three colours. Black, cyan `#7FE8DC`, red `#ED1F24`. That is the entire design |
| **Hero** | Coiled snake in cyan crosshatch/halftone with a red chromatic-aberration offset — the registration is deliberately wrong |
| **Type** | Oversized red brush script running straight off the panel edge, deliberately cropped. Vertical kanji in a red column |
| **Counterpoint** | A tiny technical footer — "FUTURE KIMONOS GLOBAL 2025 / EARN THE GLORY ©" — set at maybe 6pt against type that is 40× its size |
| **Sleeves** | Cyan bleach-wash gradient plus one red snakeskin stripe |

**The mechanics to steal:** chromatic aberration as a texture · type cropped off the edge on purpose · **extreme type-scale contrast** (enormous script against microscopic technical caption) · a three-colour ceiling.

### 3. `01-100-dogs-of-war-logo.png` — the stamp system

Brush-cut three-headed wolf inside a rough hanko-style frame with 百戦犬 set beneath. Shipped as four colourways — red on black, black on white, red on light, black on light.

**The mechanic to steal:** a mark that survives at any size in any single colour, built from brush texture rather than vector curves. Collections need a stamp, not just an illustration.

## The six design territories

Refined 6 August 2026 from Ladana's direction. These replace the earlier five.

### 1. `neo-japanese` — Neo-Japanese / Modern Bushido
Samurai, oni, dragons, tigers, snakes. Ukiyo-e and sumi-e. **Kanji and Japanese typography used as a graphic element, not as text.** Ancient mythology against modern surface. *Old Japan × future technology.*

### 2. `cyberpunk-tech` — Cyberpunk / Neo Tokyo
The strongest single influence. Red/black/white, glitch, futuristic type, vertical Japanese text, industrial graphics, **warning labels, technical information**, asymmetrical layout. Akira, Ghost in the Shell, Neo Tokyo posters, Y2K Japanese graphic design.

### 3. `luxury-streetwear` — Streetwear graphic design
**These are not sports jerseys.** Large graphics, minimal logos, negative space, one oversized focal illustration. The reference set is ACRONYM, Cav Empt, Undercover, WTAPS, Neighborhood, Nike ACG and ISPA, Stone Island Shadow Project, Guerrilla Group, C2H4, Hamcus.

### 4. `printmaking-remix` — Vintage Japanese printmaking, remixed
Ukiyo-e, Kuniyoshi, Hokusai, Yoshitoshi — **not reproduced, remixed**: paint splatter, rough halftones, distressed overlays, modern typography over historic artwork. The Takeshi piece is the house example.

### 5. `motorsport-graphics` — Motorsport graphics
The underrated influence. F1 liveries, MotoGP, Japanese drift cars, WRC decals, endurance racing. **Sponsor placement, technical numbering, asymmetry, bold type.**

### 6. `editorial-composition` — Editorial layout and combat minimalism
Two halves of one idea. **Editorial:** title, subtitle, body copy, signature, illustration, logos — a poster wrapped around a body. **Minimalism:** sleeves stay mostly clean, graphics sit naturally on the torso, branding is understated, movement lines follow body panels. *The garment is part of the composition.*

## The premium tells — read the artwork against these

What actually separates the benchmark from an average rash guard:

| Tell | Present in the benchmark | Absent in average product |
|---|---|---|
| **One hero, not many** | A single oversized focal illustration carries the whole garment | Motifs scattered across every panel with no lead |
| **Negative space** | Sleeves nearly empty. Front ghosted back. Black doing real work | Full-bleed noise edge to edge |
| **Restrained palette** | Three colours in the Serpent piece. Black anchors everything | Five-plus competing colours, rainbow gradients |
| **Layered typography** | Four type registers at four scales, deliberately overlapping | One wordmark scaled up, or default sports italic |
| **Type as graphic** | Kanji as composition, script cropped off the edge, 6pt technical caption | Text placed where text goes |
| **Texture and print artefact** | Halftone, splatter, distress, chromatic aberration, brush edges | Flat vector fills with no tonal information |
| **A complete visual world** | Palette + motif + type + label system reinforcing one narrative | A cool illustration on a shirt |
| **Front/back asymmetry** | Front quiet, back loud | Same treatment everywhere |

## The biggest thing — collections are design systems

Future Kimonos does not make cool illustrations. It builds **cohesive visual worlds**: Neo Tokyo, Immortal Samurai, Cobra 2099, Serpent Core, Blood Blossom. Each carries its own vocabulary across every garment in the drop.

A hypothetical *Cyber Dragon* collection would hold: black + cyan + red · wireframe snakes · glitch typography · industrial labels · geometric grids · chrome textures · oversized kanji · technical sleeve graphics. Every element reinforcing one narrative.

**What this means for the collector:** a find is more valuable when it demonstrates a *system* than when it shows a single nice graphic. When a find shows one brand holding a vocabulary across several pieces, say so explicitly — that is the highest-value observation available, and it is worth more than eight unrelated pretty things.

## Where to hunt — this is the actual search list

The old register hunted product feeds. **Product feeds cannot produce this.** These can.

### Japanese vintage posters and print
`japanese movie posters` · `showa graphic design` · `japanese matchbox labels` · `japanese advertising 1960s` · `vintage japanese packaging` · `japanese woodblock composition` · `japanese exhibition posters`

### Cyberpunk concept art
Syd Mead · Ghost in the Shell · Akira · Blade Runner · Cyberpunk 2077 concept art · early Beeple · Vitaly Bulgarov

### Streetwear lookbooks
**Some of the best inspiration is not on a rash guard.** ACRONYM · Guerrilla Group · C2H4 · Hamcus · Cav Empt · Undercover · Nike ISPA and ACG · Stone Island Shadow Project. **Study placement, typography and restraint** — not the garment.

### Motorsport graphics
F1 liveries · MotoGP · Japanese drift car liveries · WRC decals · endurance racing. Sponsor placement, technical numbering, asymmetry, bold type.

### Album covers
Electronic · industrial · metal · Japanese hip hop · synthwave. Designers there solve the identical problem: **one powerful graphic carrying a whole aesthetic.**

### Posters and composition theory
Swiss poster design · Japanese exhibition posters · Bauhaus · Constructivism · Brutalist graphic design. These teach hierarchy.

### Packaging
Japanese whiskey labels · sneaker boxes · perfume packaging · tea tins · sake bottles.

### Tattoo art
Irezumi · blackwork · illustrative tattoo artists · neo-Japanese tattoo artists. **The compositions translate directly to rash guards** — both are artwork fitted to a body.

### Search strings that actually work
Never search `rashguard design`. Search:

`neo japan graphic design` · `futuristic japanese poster` · `cyber samurai` · `brutalist streetwear` · `japanese editorial design` · `y2k japanese graphics` · `industrial typography` · `ukiyo-e remix` · `dystopian fashion` · `techwear graphics` · `motorsport branding` · `screenprint texture` · `japanese warning labels`

### Behance
`apparel graphics` · `sportswear design` · `technical wear` · `fashion graphics` · `print placement` · `techwear branding`

## Attribution — non-negotiable

Reference-class finds are other people's artwork. **Never file an image whose origin cannot be cited**, never present found artwork as something to copy, and always name the artist or source where it is known. The purpose is to extract the *mechanic* — the composition, the palette relationship, the type hierarchy — not the image. Write the mechanic in the note, always.
